////// Summary

The gold funerary mask of Tutankhamun, the boy king of Egypt's 18th Dynasty. He came to the throne at about nine and died at about nineteen, around 1323 BC. The mask covered the head and shoulders of his mummy, inside the innermost of three nested coffins, the last one solid gold.

Howard Carter found the tomb, KV62 in the Valley of the Kings, in November 1922, and first saw the mask on 28 October 1925, when the third coffin was opened. It is 54 cm tall and weighs about 10 kg: beaten gold inlaid with lapis lazuli, carnelian, quartz, obsidian, turquoise and coloured glass. The vulture and the cobra on the brow stand for Upper and Lower Egypt, and a spell from the Book of the Dead runs across the shoulders and the back.

////// Summary

Bronze cats like this one were offerings to Bastet, goddess of the home, of fertility and of protection, first shown as a lioness and later as a cat. Her cult centre was Bubastis in the Nile Delta. Herodotus, writing around 430 BC, describes her festival there and says it drew 700,000 pilgrims.

Most of these statues date from the Late Period (664-332 BC) and the Ptolemaic era, when cats were mummified by the hundreds of thousands as offerings; some hollow bronzes even held a cat mummy. The best known, the Gayer-Anderson cat in the British Museum (about 600 BC), wears gold rings in its ears and nose. The historian Diodorus Siculus saw a crowd in Egypt kill a Roman who had killed a cat.

////// Summary

The ankh is the hieroglyph for "life", in use since the Early Dynastic Period, around 3000 BC. In tomb and temple scenes the gods hold it to the king's nose to give him the breath of life, and it appears in royal names: Tutankhamun means "living image of Amun".

No one knows for certain what it first showed. The Egyptologist Alan Gardiner suggested a sandal strap; others see a knot of cloth or a mirror, and mirror cases were made in its shape, one of them found in Tutankhamun's tomb. Egypt's early Christians, the Copts, adopted it as the crux ansata, the cross with a handle.
